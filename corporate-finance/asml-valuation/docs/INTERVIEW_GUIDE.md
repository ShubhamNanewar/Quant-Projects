# Explaining the ASML project

## A short introduction

The project asks what operating performance would support ASML's valuation. It starts with reported US GAAP financials, builds an explicit forecast and converts operating profit into unlevered cash flow. I compare operating cases and test WACC and terminal growth. The biggest lesson is how much of the answer comes from long-term assumptions and how customer advances complicate the interpretation of cash generation.

Use this as a guide, not a script. Run the model, change the inputs and form your own view before describing it as your work.

## Questions worth practising

**Why ASML?** It links an identifiable technology position with customer investment cycles, equipment production and installed-base services. The business is interesting, but the valuation still needs assumptions about growth, margins and cash generation.

**How does it make money?** Equipment sales and net service/field-option sales. Bookings, backlog, shipments and recognised revenue are not interchangeable. Look at the annual report's revenue-recognition note before explaining the timing.

**Why use US GAAP?** It is ASML's primary reporting basis for these releases. Mixing IFRS capitalised development costs with US GAAP profit would distort comparisons.

**Reported FCF versus FCFF?** Reported FCF is operating cash flow less cash PPE and intangible purchases. Model FCFF starts with operating profit after tax, then adjusts for D&A, capex and normalised working-capital investment. They have different financing, tax, cash-timing and normalisation features.

**Why is forecast working capital positive when reported core NWC is negative?** The forecast uses a normalised funding requirement equal to 10% of sales, rather than assuming that advance payments finance an expanding negative balance forever. This is an assumption, not a granular forecast. It loses information about actual inventory and deposit timing and should be tested.

**Why 9.5% WACC?** It is an illustrative discount-rate assumption. It was not estimated from current beta, yield curves and market risk premiums. The sensitivity table makes that uncertainty visible. Do not claim that a CAPM calibration was completed.

**What is terminal value?** The present value of cash flow beyond 2031, assuming stable growth and the stated long-run operating ratios. The model recalculates terminal reinvestment. Explain why WACC must exceed terminal growth and why a high terminal-value share reduces confidence in a single price estimate.

**How do you get equity from enterprise value?** Add surplus cash and non-operating assets, subtract debt and the cash needed to operate. Here, the reserve is assumed, debt is estimated, shares use a proxy and investments are carried at book value. These are model limits, not precise fair-value measurements.

**Why does the result differ from the share price?** The market could assume stronger growth, higher margins, lower risk or a different long-run cash-flow path. A model-market gap is a starting point for investigation. It is not proof of an investment opportunity.

**Why not use the peer median as the answer?** The companies sell different equipment, have different service mixes and fiscal year-ends, and the table uses FY2025 denominators with later market prices. It is useful context, but not a matched TTM or forward valuation exercise.

**What would you improve first?** Replace the share and debt proxies; estimate a market-supported discount rate; model customer advances and inventory; update comparables to matched TTM periods. Explain which improvement could change the conclusion most.

## Practical exercise before an interview

1. Open the base case and trace revenue to FCFF to enterprise and equity value.
2. Select downside and explain the changes in margins and investment.
3. Raise WACC by one percentage point. Explain the direction and size of the result.
4. Lower the final-year gross margin by two percentage points. Observe both forecast and terminal effects.
5. Increase normalised working capital by five percentage points. Explain the investment requirement.
6. Restore the base settings and explain why source actuals did not change.

Do not claim that the analysis surprised you unless that describes your own reaction. Choose the finding you actually understand and found interesting.
