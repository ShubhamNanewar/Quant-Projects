# Explain the project in your own words

## One-minute outline

I examined whether Ford's industrial business could cover its scheduled maturities under cash-flow stress. I first separated Ford Credit from the industrial accounts, because consolidated debt and operating cash flow do not describe industrial repayment capacity. I reconciled reported cash flow, built three explicit scenarios, and measured the borrowing required to preserve a management cash buffer. The downside depends on access to facilities, and the base depends on cash conversion. I then used an illustrative bond calculation to show why repayment capacity and mark-to-market price risk are different questions. I did not conclude that a bond is cheap without market prices.

## Questions to practise

1. **Why not divide consolidated debt by automotive EBITDA?** The numerator would include finance-subsidiary liabilities funding receivables while the denominator excludes that business.
2. **Is adjusted free cash flow available for debt repayment?** Not in full. This definition excludes certain restructuring/pension outflows; distributions and other obligations also consume cash.
3. **Why is base FCF 5.5?** It is calibrated to the original guidance midpoint. Do not describe it as an independent forecast. Explain every bridge assumption, particularly other/timing.
4. **Why does production stress consume working capital?** Collections can fall while supplier invoices still mature. Timing matters; the annual model cannot capture the trough.
5. **Is $20bn a covenant?** No. It is a management cash target. The reported $4bn covenant uses a different eligible domestic cash/facility definition; this model does not calculate legal compliance.
6. **Why remove $5.5bn of facilities?** Their disclosed availability ends during 2026. No renewal or pre-expiry draw is assumed. The alternative would require explicit maturity and interest assumptions.
7. **Why divide the funding gap by 0.92?** A full-year 8% interest charge on new funding itself consumes cash. This convention is conservative and illustrative, not the actual facility rate or draw timing.
8. **What if lines are unavailable?** Required funding is capped at available facilities; the model reports the residual unmet funding need. It must not create unlimited borrowing.
9. **What could be missing in severe stress?** Ford Credit support, cash-remittance restrictions, monthly cash troughs, exceptional legal/warranty payments and cash collateral requirements.
10. **Why is yield not spread?** Yield includes the benchmark rate as well as credit/liquidity compensation. A proper spread comparison needs a consistent benchmark and actual security terms.
11. **Where is the recovery analysis?** It is not calculated. A credible waterfall needs issuer/guarantor entities, secured claims, priorities and asset values. An arbitrary recovery percentage is not evidence.
12. **What would you trade?** The case suggests investigating near-dated versus longer-duration industrial debt, but no trade is established without current relative pricing, costs and security diligence.

## Practical rehearsal

- Change the working-capital assumption by -1 and explain the change in cash and required funding.
- Set available facilities to 1 and inspect residual unmet funding.
- Remove other/timing from the base case and explain why the conclusion weakens.
- Compare a dividend suspension with capex cuts: near-term creditor benefit versus operating consequences.
- Reconcile every historical input to the annual report before submitting the report as a work sample.
